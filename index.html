How the real machine works

1Coat — spin a polymer or photoresist into a flat film on glass or silicon.
2Expose — focus a 405 nm laser to 1–5 µm and scan it with G-code over an X–Y stage.
3Develop — dissolve the dose-dependent resist to reveal the pattern.
4Inspect — measure residual thickness, line width and focus error.

    About this simulator

    Built from the two documents uploaded with this project: Building a laboratory desktop semiconductor lithography machine and DIY UV spot-line lithography machine.

    The process model uses published-style approximations: spin thickness ∝ solids/viscosity/√rpm, Gaussian beam waist with Rayleigh range, scanned-line dose, a Hill-function resist contrast curve, and an astigmatic focus-error signal.

    Mechanical parts use catalogue dimensions: 20 × 20 mm T-slot extrusion, a 42.3 mm NEMA 17 body with a Ø5 mm shaft, MGN9/MGN12 rails and Ø8 mm T8 screws. One scene unit is one millimetre. The detailed NEMA 17 and duct-fan meshes are CC0 models from Innerscene; the motor bodies stay fixed while their shafts turn.

    Motion follows the hardware: the motor body stays fixed while its shaft and coupler turn the screw, and screw angle = travel ÷ lead × 360°. Select T8x2, T8x4 or T8x8 to change lead and resolution. Spin-coat speed uses ω = 2π·rpm/60.

    Use the jog pad to move the stage, “Spin coat now” to spin the chuck, “Run job” to scan the G-code toolpath, and the auto-orbit toggle for a hands-free 3D view.

    The three photos in the source folder could not be analysed by the available models, so geometry is reconstructed from the written specifications.

    Everything runs locally in your browser. Three.js is vendored, so no network connection is required.
